A tankless water heater does not store hot water at all. It waits for a faucet to open, senses the flow, fires a burner or energizes its elements, and heats water as it races through a compact heat exchanger. That on-demand design is a big part of its appeal, but it packs a lot of sensors and controls into one small cabinet, and every one of them has to agree before you get hot water. When one of them objects, the unit shuts down and posts an error code. In Lee's Summit, MO, the usual culprits are mineral scale coating the heat exchanger, a flow sensor that no longer registers demand, an igniter or flame rod that cannot prove the flame, and venting or gas supply problems that trip the unit's safety limits.

Our approach to tankless repair is simple: measure first, replace second. When we arrive at your Lee's Summit home, we record the active code and the unit's fault history, then work through the systems behind it. Flow sensors are checked with water running to confirm they report demand correctly. Flame rods and igniters are tested electrically. Gas pressure is read at the inlet with the burner at maximum, because a line that holds steady at idle can sag badly under full load. The vent and air intake are inspected end to end, and the heat exchanger is judged by the temperature rise it achieves at a known flow. Only then do we recommend a fix, and we can show you exactly why.

Once the cause is confirmed, most repairs are finished in a single visit. We descale heat exchangers with a pump and descaling solution circulated through the service valves, replace worn flow sensors, igniters, flame rods, thermistors, and combustion fans, clean inlet filter screens, and correct venting problems that trip pressure switches. When the real issue is a gas line sized for the old tank heater, we tell you plainly and quote the correct pipe run, because no amount of part swapping will fix a unit that is starved for fuel. If you are fighting a cold-water sandwich, we explain whether it is normal behavior for your model or a sign of a setting, fixture, or recirculation problem we can correct. Signs You Need Tankless Water Heater Repair in Lee's Summit

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Hot water that cannot keep up with more than one fixture
  • The unit clicking or trying to light several times before giving up
  • No hot water at a low-flow faucet even though the shower works fine
  • Longer bursts of cold water between hot draws than you used to get
  • New whistling, roaring, or rumbling from the unit
  • An error code flashing on the unit's display
  • Outlet temperature that surges and sags while water is running
  • Moisture, rust, or drips appearing below the heater

Why Professional Tankless Water Heater Repair Matters

There are two reasons tankless repair belongs with a professional: the diagnosis is tricky and the hazards are real. On the diagnostic side, an ignition code might mean a worn igniter, a dirty flame rod, low gas pressure, or a blocked intake, and replacing parts until the code clears adds up fast. On the safety side, a gas unit burns fuel at a very high rate and vents exhaust that contains carbon monoxide, while an electric unit can pull more than 100 amps through multiple 240-volt breakers. Errors with either type can be serious.

Professional service is usually the cheaper path, too. Our technicians do not guess at error codes. They test flow sensors with water moving, measure flame rod signal, confirm igniter resistance, read inlet gas pressure at full fire, and check the vent from end to end. When scale is the problem, we flush it out properly and rinse the system before the unit goes back in service. And because some tankless problems, like an undersized gas line or a cracked heat exchanger, are not solved by parts at all, we tell you so directly, before you commit to anything.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Here are our recommendations:

  • Clean the inlet water filter screen a few times a year so the flow sensor gets full flow
  • Flush scale out of the heat exchanger annually, and sooner in hard water
  • Keep the vent termination and air intake clear of leaves, nests, snow, and lint
  • Consider a water softener or scale-reduction filter ahead of the heater if scale keeps returning
  • Treat a code that returns after a reset as a symptom worth diagnosing, not ignoring
  • Leave the power on in cold weather, since freeze protection only runs when the unit has electricity
  • Have the gas line evaluated whenever you add a range, dryer, or fireplace

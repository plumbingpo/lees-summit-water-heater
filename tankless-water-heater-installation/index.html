A tankless water heater works on a simple idea: instead of keeping 40 or 50 gallons hot around the clock, it heats water only while a tap is open. Flow through the unit trips a sensor, a powerful burner or electric element fires, and the water is heated as it passes through a compact heat exchanger. The benefits are real: no standby heat loss, hot water that does not run out during back-to-back showers, a wall-mounted unit that frees up floor space, and a service life that often runs close to two decades with maintenance. For many households in Lee's Summit, MO, that makes tankless a smart upgrade.

Sizing is where tankless installs succeed or fail. We size by flow rate and temperature rise: add up the fixtures you realistically run at the same time in gallons per minute, then factor in how cold the incoming water gets in winter, since a unit that delivers five gallons per minute at a mild rise may deliver far less when the groundwater is cold. Then comes the infrastructure. A whole-home gas unit can draw 150,000 to 199,000 BTU, which often means a larger gas line or confirming the meter can keep up. Venting must match the unit, and electric tankless models can need a large share of your electrical service, sometimes enough to require a panel upgrade.

When we install a tankless heater, we build it to be serviced. That means isolation valves for descaling, a gas line with a sediment trap sized for the burner's full demand, venting that meets the manufacturer's rules for length and termination, and a condensate line with a neutralizer on condensing models. Where water hardness is high, we explain scale protection options before the unit goes on the wall. Optional recirculation can shorten the wait at distant fixtures. We handle permits where required, test the unit at full flow, and show you how it works. Homeowners in Lee's Summit can call (816) 219-8133 for a demand assessment and a clear quote.

When to Call for Tankless Water Heater Installation

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Interest in a longer-lasting heater with replaceable parts
  • A growing household or new bathroom adding hot water demand
  • An aging tank that has started to rust, rumble, or leak
  • Showers turning lukewarm before everyone gets a turn
  • Limited floor space in a closet, garage, or utility room
  • Construction or remodeling that opens walls and simplifies the install
  • Standby losses on a tank that sits idle for long stretches
  • An addition or guest suite that is a long run from the main heater

A burner that can draw nearly 199,000 BTU needs properly sized gas piping, and leaks or undersized lines are both dangerous and costly. Venting has to match the unit exactly, with the right material, slope, and termination clearances, or combustion gases can end up where they do not belong. Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Do not cut power to a unit in a cold space, since its freeze protection needs electricity
  • Descale the heat exchanger once a year, or more often with hard water
  • Check and rinse the small filter screen at the cold inlet periodically
  • A setting near 120F limits scalding and reduces scale formation
  • Consider a water softener or scale-prevention filter if your water is hard
  • Keep the air intake and vent termination clear of snow, leaves, and debris
  • Write down any error codes and call early rather than resetting repeatedly
